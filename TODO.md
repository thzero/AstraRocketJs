# AstraRocketJs — TODO / roadmap

## Roadmap

Ordered by plausibility: how much stands between the entry and a working change, most plausible first within each group.

### Everything needed already exists

- **Tree mass rollup and stage isolation** - two small readouts on the parts tree that ZenRockets has and we do not (`zenrockets.com/docs/working-with-parts`). (a) A **sigma toggle** flipping each row's mass between THIS part's mass and its whole subtree's total, so a nose cone row can read either its own shell or the shell plus the chute, shock cord and nose weight inside it. (b) A **stage-isolation** dropdown that recomputes mass, CG, CP and stability for one stage alone rather than the assembled stack - which is how you check whether a spent booster is stable on its own way down, and the question a multi-stage design most needs to ask and currently cannot. Both are read-only views over data we already have: `ComponentTree.tsx` / `TreePanel.tsx` render the rows, `buildRocket.ts` already assembles the tree for the kernel, and the stage case is a filtered tree through the same static-analysis path the stability badge uses. Neither touches the engine.

### Languages

- **Twenty-five more languages.** The ten that ship (en, de, es, fr, pt-BR, pt-PT, nl, pl, ru, ja) proved the mechanism, and `locales.test.ts` now holds each locale to the plural categories its own `Intl.PluralRules` declares rather than to English's two, which was the only thing structurally in the way. Everything below is additive content on machinery that already works. Grouped by the number of plural forms the language needs, because that is the only thing that varies per file:
  - **1 form** (`other` only), 1248 keys each: `zh-Hans`, `zh-Hant`, `ko`, `id`, `vi`, `th`, `ms`. No `_one` at all - the test rejects one as a dead string nothing selects.
  - **2 forms** (`one`, `other`), 1256 keys: `tr`, `sv`, `nb`, `da`, `el`, `hu`, `fi`, `fil`, `bn`, `bg`. Same shape as English, German and Dutch.
  - **3 forms**, 1264 keys: `ro`, `sr`, `hr` take `one`/`few`/`other`; `ca` takes `one`/`many`/`other` like Spanish and Portuguese.
  - **4 forms** (`one`, `few`, `many`, `other`), 1272 keys: `cs`, `uk`, `sk`, `lt`. Same class as Polish and Russian, and the same trap: a missing category does not degrade to that file's `_other`, it falls through to ENGLISH, so a gap shows as English text inside a translated UI.

  **Four naming decisions to settle before writing any file**, all the same class of decision as pt-BR vs pt-PT, and all of them cheap now and expensive later. (a) Chinese is a SCRIPT split, not a regional one, so `zh-Hans` / `zh-Hant` rather than `zh-CN` / `zh-TW`; order matters in `LOCALES`, because a browser asking for bare `zh` lands on whichever `zh-*` row is listed first, exactly as bare `pt` lands on `pt-BR`. (b) "Norwegian" is two written standards: `nb` (Bokmål, what almost everyone writes) and `nn` (Nynorsk). Shipping `nb` alone means deciding what bare `no` and an explicit `nn` resolve to. (c) Tagalog wants `fil` (Filipino), which is what `Intl` and most browsers use, but some send `tl`; pick one and let the other widen onto it. (d) "Serbian/Croatian" is not one locale but at least two, `sr` and `hr`, and Serbian is itself written in two scripts, so `sr-Cyrl` and `sr-Latn` may make it three files. Any region-tagged or script-tagged pair also needs its cases pinned in `resolve.test.ts`, which is where the pt pair's widening rules live.

  **What does NOT need work:** none of the twenty-five is right-to-left, so there is no bidi or mirrored-layout job hiding here - that would have been the one item bigger than the translation itself. Glyph coverage comes from the OS through the `system-ui` stack in `index.html` rather than a bundled webfont, which already carries Cyrillic and Japanese, so Chinese, Korean, Greek, Thai and Bengali need no font work either. Worth eyeballing the dense table headers (`dash.colBurn`, `dash.colMassFrac`) and the fixed-width export language `<select>` in CJK and Thai, where the same meaning arrives in far fewer or far taller glyphs.

  **Per language the mechanical cost is six edits:** the JSON file, one row in `LOCALES` (`web/src/i18n/index.ts`), one row in `LOCALES` in `locales.test.ts`, one in `componentFields.options.test.ts`, the expected option list in `FlightPathExport.test.tsx`, and the native switcher name in `.cspell/project-words.txt` when cspell does not know the word. A generator is worth writing rather than hand-editing 25 files: walk `en.json` for the key set and key ORDER, take a flat `{"a.b": "text"}` map, emit the target locale's plural categories in CLDR order, and fail on a missing, extra, empty or placeholder-mismatched key. That is how the last four were built, and it means a file lands already passing the suite instead of being debugged into it.

  **The real cost is not the first write, it is every UI string after it.** At ten locales a new English key has to land in ten files or the suite goes red, which is already the dominant tax on any string change; at thirty-five it decides whether adding a label is a five-minute job. Settle the workflow before the volume, not after: hand-authoring twenty-five more files is plausible once and unmaintainable as a habit, so this probably wants either a machine-translation first pass with native review per language, or a translation platform that owns the files, with `locales.test.ts` staying as the gate either way. Adding languages faster than they can be kept current produces ten stale translations, which is worse than four current ones.

### Contained, but real work

- **Linked dimensions** - let a fit dimension REFERENCE another part's dimension instead of being typed. ZenRockets (`zenrockets.com/docs/sizing-positioning`) puts a small "A" button on every field that supports it: link a coupler's outside diameter to the body tube's inside diameter and it recalculates whenever the tube changes, with guards against circular references and impossible geometry. We are fully parametric already, but every fit value is hand-typed, so resizing a body tube silently leaves its coupler, centering rings, engine block and motor mount at the old bore and nothing flags it. `ScaleDialog` scaling the whole rocket at once is the blunt version of the same need. Obvious link targets: coupler / centering-ring / bulkhead OD to the parent tube's ID, inner-tube OD to the centering ring's ID, motor-mount ID to the mounted motor's diameter, transition fore/aft radius to the neighboring tube radii, fin root chord to the parent tube's length. To build: a `link?: { field, nodeId, sourceField }` on the node (or a `links` map beside the dimensions), resolution in `buildRocket.ts` before the tree reaches the kernel, cycle detection at set time, a linked/unlinked affordance in `DimensionFields.tsx` showing the resolved value read-only, and `orkFile.ts` import/export as a custom element - OpenRocket has no such concept, so a desktop round-trip drops the link and keeps the resolved number, which is acceptable but belongs in the docs.

- **Batch simulate - fly a set of motors against one design.** "Which of these do I fly today" is a question a single Launch cannot answer: you would build a flight configuration per candidate by hand, run them, and compare the rows yourself. The feature is a mount, a set of candidate motors, one run, and a table sorted by whatever you care about - apogee, rail-exit speed, max velocity, time to apogee, descent rate. mmrocket-sim ships this; it is the one thing of theirs that looks worth having. **Approved 2026-10-06 as the next piece of work**; nothing outside the code blocks it.

  **Most of the machine is already here, in the wind sweep.** `store.runDriftSweep` is a many-flights-one-question runner already: it fans out over `simClient.simulateInWorker` across `simConcurrency()` workers, holds an `AbortController` so a new ask supersedes the one in flight, reports `{ done, total }` progress rather than per-flight noise, and refuses up front with the SAME `designBlocker` / `unflyable` checks the Run button uses, so a batch cannot fly what a single run would not. Per-row preferences resolve the same way (`{ ...prefs, ...sim.prefs }`) and `freshSeed()` fills a missing seed. On the output side `flightColumns.ts` and `csvExport.ts` already hold the column vocabulary a run table downloads with, so the table has a format before it has a screen.

  **What is NOT like theirs, and is the thing to settle: our motors live in flight configurations.** Theirs sit on the design, so sweeping a mount is swapping a field. Here every candidate motor IS a configuration, which forces a choice: a batch either CREATES configurations - persisted, named, and leaving thirty rows in the Simulations table that nobody asked to keep - or it flies throwaway ones that never reach the store. The second, for the reason `driftSweep` already gives in its own comment: a few dozen flights answering a question about the row you are reading right now should not become a few dozen things to tidy up afterwards. So the result is a transient table beside the sweep, not new rows, and it is dropped with the workspace exactly as a sweep is.

  **Scope to state up front, because theirs is wider.** One mount per batch, with every other mount held at the active configuration's motors. No mixed-cluster combinations: our cluster is one motor choice for the whole ring (`cluster` on the inner tube), not a per-tube pick, so the combinatorics theirs has do not exist here. Candidates filtered by that mount's own bore and length through `motorPicker.motorFitsMount` and `mountFit`, which is now the single place that judges whether a motor goes in a tube.

  Three things to decide before starting: where it lives (a row in the Motors panel, or its own pane beside Results); whether the table downloads the way the run table does (`runTableCsv` in `csvExport.ts`, which already writes these columns); and whether a row can be PROMOTED into a real simulation once you pick a winner, which is the one thing that would justify writing configurations after all, for one row rather than thirty.

- **Saved parts as a file** (from the MMRocket Sim review, 2026-10-07). MMRocket Sim imports and exports your own parts as CSV. **Save as part** keeps them in this browser only, so they cannot move to another device or to anyone else. Desktop OpenRocket has no in-app import or export for parts, so nothing there sets the format; if parts are ever shared as files, use desktop's `.orc` (the format of its parts database, which `scripts/sync-components.mjs` already reads), not CSV, so a file made here also loads into desktop's parts library.

### Blocked on something outside the code

- **Support camera shrouds & protuberances** — external add-ons (camera pods, avionics fairings, rail-standoffs). Today they're only vestigial: a mention in the dead `tree/schema.ts` and some stray render scaffolding in `TreeSchematic`, but they're not a real `ComponentType`, can't be added (`treeEdit.ts`), can't be edited (`PropertyPanel.tsx`), and don't import/export (`orkFile.ts`) — nothing can actually create one. NOTE: these are **not** OpenRocket base-model components, so the kernel won't simulate them as first-class aero bodies — treat like the app's `fairing` extension (custom `.ork` element, mass + drawing, approximate/zero aero). To support: add the `ComponentType`(s), wire `treeEdit` (allowed children + defaults), give `PropertyPanel` their fields (dimensions + **angle around body**, reusing the `'angle'` field kind), add `orkFile` import/export as a custom element, and finish the side/aft/3D render paths (honoring the angle, like launch lugs now do).

- **Forecast atmosphere aloft is ours until upstream #2737 lands.** Upstream has no atmosphere but `ExtendedISAModel`, so the forecast's temperature, pressure and humidity at each pressure level fly through `engine-java/src/api/java/api/AtmosphereProfile.java`, an `AtmosphericModel` the bridge plugs in (no patched kernel file). `LaunchConditions.atmosphereLevels` carries it, and a `.ork` saves it as `<forecastlevel>` children of `<atmosphere>`, an extension desktop skips. Parity scenarios `flight.atmo.*` hold it on JVM, JS and WASM. When OpenRocket ships its own model (issue `openrocket/openrocket#2737`): build theirs in the bridge instead, delete `AtmosphereProfile`, read `<forecastlevel>` on import and write their element on export, and compare their interpolation (ours is linear temperature and humidity, log-linear pressure, standard-atmosphere shape outside the levels) before switching, since the golden lines will move.

- **Flight-log import (MAYBE - see the format caveat)** - read an altimeter / flight-computer CSV as a *recorded flight* and put it in the same charts, 3D path and ground track as a simulation, so a design can be checked against what actually flew. ZenRockets (`zenrockets.com/docs/flight-data-import`) is the reference for the shape: the minimum input is a time column plus either altitude or pressure, and every other channel only sharpens the reconstruction. Their pipeline is (a) pressure to altitude through the 7-layer ISA, skipping invalid samples, (b) vertical velocity by Savitzky-Golay smoothing plus analytic differentiation when altitude is all there is, or a Kalman filter that estimates accelerometer bias from the pad period and cross-checks against the barometric profile when an accel channel exists (falling back to altitude-only if the two disagree), (c) horizontal track from measured x/y first, then GPS lat/lon with short gaps interpolated, then a synthetic wind-drift model, (d) every channel resampled onto the vertical profile's timestamps, (e) auto-detection of liftoff, burnout, apogee and landing, with deployments NEVER inferred - the user marks drogue/main by hand.
  **Why this is a maybe:** there is no standard. Every altimeter vendor (Altus Metrum, PerfectFlite, Eggtimer, Featherweight, Missile Works, Jolly Logic, Blue Raven, ...) writes its own CSV with its own column names, units and header junk, and several ship binary or vendor-tool-only formats that never reach a CSV at all. A parser per vendor is an unbounded maintenance tail that ages badly. The only version worth building is the GENERIC one: one CSV reader plus a column-mapping step where the user says which column is time, which is altitude or pressure, which is acceleration, and picks the unit per column - with header auto-detection as a convenience and the mapping saved as a named **profile** so it is a one-time cost per altimeter, not per file. Settle that before starting: a mapper is a week of work, a vendor zoo is forever.
  Most of the downstream half already exists: `flightChartTraces.ts buildTraces` already draws several flights at once with per-series colors, `FlightChartPanel` / `FlightChart` / `FlightChartEvents` own the axis, zoom and event strip, `FlightPath3D` and `GroundTrack` already replay and plot a track, `interpolate.ts lerpAt` samples a series at a time, and `flightColumns.ts` / `csvExport.ts` already hold the column vocabulary in the other direction. The missing piece is a reader that produces a `FlightResult`-shaped object (`series` + `events` + `summary`) so everything downstream takes it unchanged. The kernel has an ISA atmosphere, but a pure-JS 7-layer ISA is ~30 lines and keeps the importer off the engine boundary and unit-testable.
  If it lands, the per-flight **time offset** from `zenrockets.com/docs/comparing-flights` comes with it: a recorded log starts whenever the altimeter woke up, so each flight in an overlay needs its own offset (theirs is 0.1 s steps, persisted) to line liftoff or burnout up with a simulation. Cheap, and the overlay is unreadable without it.

- **A caution band between "fine" and "too fast" on the deployment warnings - wanted upstream first.** mmrocket-sim warns in three levels where we warn in two: between a deployment speed that is fine and one that is flagged it has a middle band saying the number is survivable but worth a look, with its main-chute tiers at 70 and 90 ft/s. Ours are single thresholds (`deploymentSpeedWarn`, `mainHighSpeedWarn`, `mainLowSpeedWarn`, `drogueLowSpeedWarn` in `SimPrefs`, one row each in Settings, Simulation), so a deployment is either silent or warned and nothing sits in between. **The mechanism is not the work.** `SimSummary.tsx` already picks a tile tone from the threshold and `warningText.ts` already renders a string per warning, so a third tone and a second number per band is a small change. **The numbers are the work, and they are not ours to choose.** A speed that says "this will probably hurt your airframe" is a claim about real hardware, and inventing a second one puts our name on advice desktop OpenRocket does not give. So this waits on OpenRocket carrying a banded warning of its own, which the kernel would then hand us the way it hands us the ones we show now. If it lands upstream: a settings row per band, a tone, and ten translations.

- **A weighed pad mass for a motor - wanted upstream first.** mmrocket-sim lets you enter what a motor actually weighed on the scale, with its case, closure and adapter, and flies that instead of the catalog figure. The case for it is reloadables: a catalog row's total weight is the LOADED MOTOR, while what goes in the rocket is that plus the case, the closure and often an adapter, which on a 38 mm reload is easily a hundred grams, all of it at the tail. Loaded mass, CG and the stability margin all move. On a single-use Estes motor it is noise.

  **OpenRocket cannot do it, and that is the blocker.** A motor's mass is read straight off its thrust curve: `ThrustCurveMotor.getLaunchMass()` is `cg[0].getWeight()` and `getBurnoutMass()` the last point, with no setter and no override flag; `MotorConfiguration` carries none either; and `MassCalculator` keeps `calculateStructure` and `calculateMotor` apart, so a component mass override with "use instead of everything inside" never reaches the motor seated in it.

  **And it is not ours to add.** This app is a UI over the engine, and what a motor weighs and where it balances are figures the kernel states. Adjusting them on the way in would make this app the thing that decides them, which is the line the project does not cross whatever the arithmetic looks like. It is the same rule that keeps CP, CG, calibers and the stability margin coming from the kernel rather than from a formula here.

  So: only if OpenRocket carries a motor mass AND CG override of its own. Then it is a per-mount field, the value passed through, and the usual ten translations.

## Not doing

Decided against, with the reason, so they are not proposed again. Move one back to the Roadmap if the reason stops holding.

- **A curated catalog of real club fields** - saved launch locations already cover naming the field you fly from and recalling its coordinates and elevation (`services/storage/launchLocationStore.ts`, `components/sim/LocationPicker.tsx`), including entering one from the map when it has no published numbers. Shipping a LIST of real fields is a sourcing-and-freshness commitment rather than a code one. If it is ever reconsidered it fits `remoteData.ts` as a `sites.generated.json` under `public/data/`, refreshable from the `data` branch like the motor and component catalogs. The **Real weather** entry names the same thing as the PR's `SavedPadRepository` half; that half is covered by the locations we already ship.

- **A separate "Fly screen" for the phone** (seen in MMRocket Sim, skipped 2026-10-06) - a second phone app beside the current phone layout, with its own key numbers, motor row, launch inputs and Launch button. The phone Results screen is already settled, safety card above the tiles included, and two phone layouts would be two to keep right.

- **XLSX export with native Excel charts** (seen in MMRocket Sim, skipped 2026-10-06) - a heavy dependency for what the CSV exports already carry. Any spreadsheet charts a CSV.

- **A paged on-screen table of the raw flight samples** (seen in MMRocket Sim, skipped 2026-10-06) - the flight data CSV is those samples, and a few thousand rows on screen add little to it.

- **Two-axis comparison charts** (seen in MMRocket Sim, skipped 2026-10-06) - the flight chart is small multiples on purpose: measures of different size each get their own scale rather than sharing a dual axis. Velocity against altitude, the comparison worth having, is its own panel.

- **Placing rail buttons automatically, and a warning when a fin, lug or button sits on the rail line** (seen in MMRocket Sim, skipped 2026-10-06) - app-side geometry that desktop OpenRocket's component dialogs do not have, and those dialogs are the spec here.

- **Upper-stage ignition defaults by propellant** (seen in MMRocket Sim, skipped 2026-10-06) - burnout plus 1 s for composite and Automatic for black powder. Changing OpenRocket's defaults would make the same `.ork` fly differently here than on the desktop.

- **A draggable, foldable stats card** (seen in MMRocket Sim, skipped 2026-10-06) - the Info card already toggles on and off, and dragging it adds little.

- **A graded pass/fail safety grid** (seen in MMRocket Sim, not taken up 2026-10-06) - lift-off speed, 5 : 1 thrust to weight, deploy speed tiers, descent rates, margin and weathercocking as marks. It would compare kernel figures with safety-code limits, which is allowed, but it overlaps the kernel flight warnings and the threshold settings, and weathercocking would need a formula of its own. If it is ever wanted, fold the caution tier into the existing warnings rather than add a second panel.

- **A density altitude readout** (seen in MMRocket Sim, skipped 2026-10-06) - density altitude is a pilot's figure, and desktop OpenRocket does not show it. Showing it would mean a second atmosphere formula in the app beside the kernel's. The Environment view already gives the kernel's own air density at launch, which is the number that changes the flight.

- **A longitude check that offers to flip east and west** (seen in MMRocket Sim, skipped 2026-10-06) - it compares the site with the file's other sites, or with the fetched ground height, and offers to change the sign. The latitude and longitude fields now name the hemisphere beside the number as you type (`components/sim/LatLonRows.tsx`), and the site map shows where the point is, which covers the dropped minus sign the check exists for without the extra machinery.

- **A whole-app crash recovery screen** (seen in MMRocket Sim, skipped 2026-10-07) - a guard around the whole app that, on a rendering error, offers to download the autosaved design as `.ork` and start fresh. Only the lazily loaded views have a guard today, so an error elsewhere blanks the window, but nobody has seen it happen, and a reload brings back the autosave.

- **"On a fin" and "Between fins" angle buttons, and a warning when a part sits in front of a fin** (seen in MMRocket Sim, skipped 2026-10-07) - app-side geometry that desktop OpenRocket's component dialogs do not have, the same as the rail button placement above.

- **A parachute spill hole field** (seen in MMRocket Sim, skipped 2026-10-07) - desktop's field is commented out of its parachute dialog ("TODO COMPLETE Spill hole development") and the kernel has no such property, so it is a value that only lives in files. `.ork` import and export carry it through untouched.

- **"Motor case is the airframe", and a maximum motor length per mount with an Estimate button** (seen in MMRocket Sim, skipped 2026-10-07) - neither is in desktop OpenRocket's dialogs.

- **Filling a part's fields from its catalog entry when a file opens, with "use" marks and a Detach button** (seen in MMRocket Sim, skipped 2026-10-07) - desktop does not fill fields from the catalog on open; it links the part and drops the link when a dimension is edited, which the app already does (`treeEdit.breaksPreset`).

- **A weighed mass and balance point that places ballast at the right station** (seen in MMRocket Sim, skipped 2026-10-07) - the ballast position is a calculation the app would own, and desktop answers a weighed rocket with mass and CG overrides. A `.rkt` known mass already becomes a stage override, as desktop's RockSim import does.

- **A list of real canopies ranked by descent rate and filtered by bay fit** (seen in MMRocket Sim, skipped 2026-10-07) - the ranking is a descent calculation of the app's own. The parachute catalog in the parts picker and the Parachute sizing tool cover choosing a canopy.

- **Standard tube diameters, snapping mounts to motor sizes, a motor-fit warning and a backup offer in the Scale dialog** (seen in MMRocket Sim, skipped 2026-10-07) - not in desktop OpenRocket's Scale dialog, whose options (scope, from/to, explicit masses, offsets) the app now has.

- **Snapping to the parent's middle and to fin tab edges** (seen in MMRocket Sim, skipped 2026-10-07) - desktop's only snapping is for its measuring calipers.

- **A first-run tour** (seen in MMRocket Sim, skipped 2026-10-07) - it has to be redone every time the layout changes, and the docs and in-app Help cover getting started without that upkeep.

- **Diameter or radius entry, and a choice of stability in calibers, percent or both** (seen in MMRocket Sim, skipped 2026-10-07) - desktop has no radius toggle, and the app already shows calibers and percent together, so a choice could only take one away.

- **Whole-rocket OBJ, GLB and STL export** (seen in MMRocket Sim, skipped 2026-10-07) - whole-rocket 3MF and every format per part already cover printing; a whole-rocket display model adds little.

- **Splitting oversized parts for a 3D printer** (seen in MMRocket Sim, skipped 2026-10-07) - printer bed presets, joint clearance and a zip of pieces joined by spigots: a large feature for a narrow need.

- **Share links that carry the design in the URL** (seen in MMRocket Sim, skipped 2026-10-07) - sharing a design means sending its `.ork`, which every OpenRocket user can open. A link would hold the design after the `#`, which never reaches a server, but long designs make long links, and opening one has to ask before replacing the design on screen.

- **A "Keep this tab's design" choice when two tabs edit the same design** (seen in MMRocket Sim, skipped 2026-10-07) - it would overwrite the other tab's newer save. The banner that points to export or reopen loses nothing.

- **In-app release notes** (seen in MMRocket Sim, skipped 2026-10-07) - CHANGELOG.md already records each release for users.

- **A run history kept across motor swaps** (seen in MMRocket Sim, skipped 2026-10-07) - the run table exports every simulation in the design, and a flight worth keeping can be kept as a simulation of its own.

- **Flying the rounded optimum ejection delay automatically, with a report of delays by mount** (seen in MMRocket Sim, skipped 2026-10-07) - desktop OpenRocket shows the optimum delay and leaves the delay to the flier, as the app does.

- **A "check thrustcurve.org" button listing new, changed and withdrawn motors** (seen in MMRocket Sim, skipped 2026-10-07) - not in desktop, and the motor catalog already refreshes from the `data` branch on a schedule without a rebuild.

- **Quick picks of common Estes motors that fit the mount** (seen in MMRocket Sim, skipped 2026-10-07) - not in desktop, and the motor picker's "Fits the mount" filter does the same job.

- **An estimated cloud base in the weather dialog** (seen in MMRocket Sim, skipped 2026-10-07) - worked out from the temperature and dew-point spread, a weather formula of the app's own, and a rough one from model-grid values; the same reason as the density altitude readout. The dialog shows the forecast's own cloud cover and visibility instead.

- **Quoting 14 CFR 101.25 beside the forecast's cloud and visibility** (seen in MMRocket Sim, skipped 2026-10-07) - legal text to keep current, and a quoted rule beside a forecast number reads as a check even when labeled otherwise. The safety notes already defer to the safety code, the field's waiver and the RSO.

- **RockSim simulation import** - a `.rkt`'s motor SELECTION does not come across: reading `EngineSet` / `StageNEngines` so the motors seat themselves on the matching mount would need the serial-number cross-reference (`MountSerialNo`) that neither `services/files/rktImport.ts` nor `services/files/rktExport.ts` writes. The design itself moves both ways, and the limitation is documented where a user meets it: the FAQ and **Files & Exports** both say motors and launch conditions stay behind, because RockSim keeps those with its simulations rather than its designs. Pick a motor after importing.
