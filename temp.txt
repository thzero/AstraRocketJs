Weather for your launch, from Open-Meteo
Get weather, at the top of Launch Conditions and on the phone Fly screen, fetches one hour's weather for one place from Open-Meteo, a free weather service, shows it beside what the panel holds now, and changes nothing until you press Apply. Type a town ("Gerlach, NV"), a US ZIP code, or coordinates - decimal, degrees-minutes-seconds, or a map link with them in it (a Google Maps link from the address bar has them; a short share link does not, and the dialog says so) - or press Use my location. Pick a date and an hour, listed in the site's time zone, and the review shows the temperature, station pressure and 10 m wind beside your current values, with the place's latitude and longitude. The rows arrive ticked; untick any you do not want, and Apply writes only the ones left ticked, plus Site altitude if you choose the ground height (below). Cancel, closing the dialog or any failure writes nothing. A strip under the fields then names the place and hour the numbers came from, each field says where its own number came from, and the strip carries Undo and Dismiss.

The pressure is STATION pressure, the barometer on the pad at the altitude the flight flies - never the sea-level figure a weather app shows. That makes Site altitude matter. Left at its default of 0 it would fetch sea-level air for a high pad, so the app also asks for the ground height at the place, and where it differs from your Site altitude by more than 30 m, fetches the air at both and lets you choose. Change Site altitude afterwards and the strip says the applied air was for the old one, and offers to fetch again.

What it gives you and what it does not: the wind is the forecast's 10 m (33 ft) value at that hour, an instantaneous one, at a single level. The gust and the compass direction the wind comes from are shown but never applied - the app's wind always blows from one fixed direction - so set Rod aim (below) yourself for how your rail leans against it. It reaches 16 days ahead, counting today, and 92 days back, and for an earlier date, back to 1940, it uses Open-Meteo's historical archive: the weather as it was, not a forecast, for re-flying a past launch. A town search finds the town center, not your field, and the review says so; for a field out of town, paste the field's coordinates. Postal-code search works mainly in the US. It needs a connection, but weather already applied is ordinary launch conditions and flies offline.

What leaves your browser, and where it goes: a place name you search for, with the country set in the dialog, goes to Open-Meteo's place search, and the chosen place's coordinates, the dates around the day you pick and the altitudes to fetch the air for go to Open-Meteo for the weather. Nothing about your rocket is sent. Like any web request, each one reaches Open-Meteo from your IP address, and what they do with it is in their own terms, linked from the guide's "What leaves your browser" paragraph. "Use my location" asks your browser first and rounds the position to 0.01°, about 1 km, before it is sent or kept, because latitude and longitude travel inside any .ork or share link made from the design.

Wind gusts σ from the forecast gust
A forecast gives a gust, not a σ. Once a forecast's wind is applied, a row under the wind fields offers an estimate, and writes it only when you press "Estimate from forecast gust". The estimate is σ ≈ (gust - average) ÷ 3: Open-Meteo's gust is the strongest in the hour before, and over an hour the largest of many gusts sits about three standard deviations above the average. A 5 m/s wind gusting 11 m/s gives 2 m/s. Treat it as good to about ±25 percent - a rule of thumb, not a measurement. When σ comes out above 30 percent of the average wind, the row warns that the gust is far above the average, which usually means thermals or showers rather than steady turbulence, where the estimate is least reliable. σ is capped at the average wind, the most desktop OR's own panel allows. What σ gives you is one plausible gusty flight, not a landing spread, because every flight in the app uses one fixed gust history - and the simulated gusts are much quicker than real ones.

Density altitude
Launch Conditions now show the density altitude of the air the flight flies at the pad, worked out from the Temperature and Station pressure the flight uses. It is a readout, not a setting, and moves no number. At a 4,000 ft field on a 95 °F day with the pressure left blank, it reads 7,122 ft, 3,122 ft above the site. It is not cut off at zero, which makes one mistake easy to see: typing a sea-level altimeter setting, 1,013.25 hPa, at a 1,190 m (3,904 ft) pad reads -933 ft, beside the panel's caution about that pressure. It is dry-air density altitude, with no humidity term. And it is not the same as launching from that altitude: the speed of sound still follows the real temperature, and a motor's pressure thrust the real pressure. Each run stores the density altitude it flew in; the launch report shows it, and the Run table download carries it as a new last column, so every existing column keeps its place.

Rod aim and Longitude
Rod aim says which way a tilted rod leans relative to the wind. Its default, 0°, leans the rod straight into the wind, which is how every flight flew before, so nothing moves until you change it. 180° leans it downwind and ±90° across the wind, positive to your right as you face into it; on a vertical rod it does nothing. Leaning into the wind and letting the rocket weathercock usually gives the lowest apogee and the least drift, and leaning downwind lets the wind stand it back up, so it climbs higher and drifts further. On a small test rocket with a test motor labelled C6, a 5° rod and a steady 4 m/s wind: 321 m of apogee and 305 m of drift aimed into the wind, 326 m and 359 m across it, 331 m and 408 m downwind. Rod aim travels in an .ork and in share links. To make room the Launch panel is reordered: Rod angle and Rod aim share the top row, Rod length moves down beside Site altitude, Density altitude sits beside Time step, and Latitude moves to the bottom row beside a new Longitude field.

Longitude by itself changes the flight's position offset, not its dynamics, and it travels with the .ork and the flight-data download. East is positive and west negative, so a map's 119.06° W is -119.06; blank flies -80.6°, desktop OR's default. But it was wrong to say that a wrong sign moves no flight number: Get weather offers "This design's site" and fetches for the stored coordinates. Applying that weather changes the air and wind the rocket flies in. Check the hemisphere before fetching weather, and type a west longitude negative. A warning that asks before changing a suspicious sign has been researched but is not built. Saving an .ork from the app writes the panel's one site into every simulation, so it also removes those differing sites from the saved copy.

Feedback on how the weather defaults and Open-Meteo data work at your field is welcome.

The guide now has a glossary
Open the user guide and choose Glossary at the end of its contents. Its 253 entries give the meaning, the exact label and place you meet it in the app, and a caveat where it affects what you type or trust. It covers physics terms, the app's labels, file types and export symbols. The guide cannot jump between entries, so use your browser's find, Ctrl+F or Cmd+F on a Mac, to reach a term.

Feedback is welcome on Glossary entries.

Recovery sizing now uses the correct Cd
A parachute whose Cd is left blank flies at the simulator's automatic 0.8, reduced for its spill hole if it has one. Before, the size line could borrow the other chute's Cd. The line now uses the chute's own Cd, or its automatic value.
A chute with no Canopy diameter now ranks as 0.3 m, which is the kernel default. Its empty diameter box now shows the 0.3 m default and the arrows step from it. The panel also stops calling a role empty when it holds a chute or a streamer; it says whose Cd the line uses and whether that role has a parachute.
The Scale tool now scales that missing diameter from 0.3 m too. It used to leave the canopy at 0.3 m while scaling its spill hole: a Cd 1.5 chute with a 0.1 m hole went from a flown Cd of 1.33 to 0.83 on a 2x scale. This can change a flight, when you scale a chute without a diameter. Re-open the unscaled design and scale it again to get the corrected canopy. A better option is to relook at all your chutes after scaling to ensure they make sense.

Recovery sizing could list canopies too small for a high field
With one of Temperature or Station pressure typed and the other left blank, the Recovery sizing panel filled the blank with the sea-level standard, while the flight has filled it for your site altitude since v0.122. The panel and the flight now read the pad through one function, and a test holds the two together for every combination of blank and typed values. If you chose a canopy from the sizing panel for a site above sea level with only one of temperature and pressure filled in, open the design and read the list again.
The panel also listed wrong rates in three other cases:
With the main or drogue slot empty, every candidate was rated without its own weight. Each candidate now carries its own weight. If you picked a canopy from the list for an empty slot, read the list again.
Under a stage mass override that covers everything inside it each candidate was rated with its own weight swapped in, although a heavier or lighter canopy changes nothing the simulation flies.
A chute inside a pod set was sized to carry the whole rocket. The panel now says when a size is per canopy.
Fixed stability bugs
A part a file places at an absolute station inside a pod set or strap-on was moved when the design opened.
A protuberance's mass was flown at its leading edge, or its trailing edge when positioned from the bottom, rather than at its centre.
A camera shroud of exactly zero height, which the slider's left stop could set, built and flew the wrong numbers. The height is now stored at 0.1 mm.
A catalogue link could turn a hollow nose or transition solid.
A .CDX1 booster fin set on any tube but the booster's last moved aft on a save and re-open. It now stays put.
A .rkt save put a mass component or camera shroud at its front end rather than its balance point.
RockSim .rkt import fixes
It opens on one of the simulations the file stores, with each of the others as a flight configuration. The app used to merge every simulation's motors into one set, the last simulation to name a mount winning, which could make a pairing none of them flies.
A plugged motor no longer fires an ejection charge it does not have. RockSim writes -2 for plugged and -1 for its run that flies every listed delay, and the app read both as seconds, so a chute on the ejection charge of a plugged motor came out at burnout.
Air starts, weighed clusters and mixed clusters fly as the file says. RockSim counts the ignition delay of the stage that leaves the pad from launch, and the app dropped it, so an air-start motor lit at liftoff. A cluster weighed tube by tube was flown with one tube's weight for the whole cluster. And identical tubes that the file's simulations load with different motors are now kept as separate mounts.
Imported files load the motors by name more often
This applies to .rkt, .ork and .CDX1 alike. The app finds a file's motor by the name the file gives it, and it went wrong in several ways. It matched a name that was only the start of another, cutting a number in two: RockSim's Cesaroni "G115-WT" loaded as AeroTech's 29 mm G11, and a "J100" as Loki's J1000. It could not find a Cesaroni motor named by its common name and propellant, such as "J530-IM" or "N5800-CS", nor one in RockSim's impulse-first Cesaroni form, nor a Loki or AMW motor with its propellant suffix, such as Loki's "J525LW". And when two manufacturers had a motor with the same name, often the wrong one was chosen: if the file names an Estes "A3T", Quest's A3T was loaded, and "E15" under AeroTech loaded RV's E15. Now a name no longer matches by cutting a number in two, a common name followed by that motor's propellant finds it, a leading impulse is read past, and the maker written in the file wins. Where the app still loads a motor the file did not confirm - another maker's, one of several that match equally well, or the closest to a name it cannot fully read - the note now says what it loaded and asks you to check it. Before, each of these loaded without a word.

A motor the app cannot match now leaves the mount empty, and the note says it matched nothing, instead of a wrong motor loading silently.

A design opened in an earlier version keeps the motor it was given then, in the browser and in any file saved from it. IF A DESIGN CAME FROM A .rkt, .ork OR .CDX1, OPEN THE ORIGINAL FILE AGAIN, and where a note says a motor loaded without the file confirming it, check that it is the motor you expected, or pick another with the Browse motors button on the mount's card in the Motors panel.

Off-centre inner tubes now count toward roll inertia
For any inner tube off the centreline, with or without a motor in it. A split cluster - each motor in its own tube, placed with Distance off centerline - was given the roll inertia of the tubes stacked on the axis.

Mass, CG and pitch inertia do not move, and an uncanted flight has no roll moment, so no apogee changes unless your fins are canted. The change is in the mass model all three aerodynamics models share, so Classic Extended Barrowman now shows a higher roll inertia than desktop OR 24.12 for these designs.

Other numbers that moved
A design with pods, or with a step where a wider part meets a narrower one, flies a little lower once its stage has a nozzle exit diameter, under Rogers Modified Barrowman (Kbf), Auto and Supersonic. While a motor burns, the app takes the nozzle exit area off the base drag, and it took it off every base in the stage - each pod's tube and any step-down with no transition - although the pressure thrust the exit adds is charged once. It now comes off the stage's aft-most base only. No file moves just by opening, but Batch simulate flies every candidate with its published exit, so batch rows for these designs move. Classic Extended Barrowman is unchanged.
A motor in a pod set or on a strap-on was counted once, not once per pod, in half the app - the nozzle autofill, the nozzle size check, the stored run, Batch and the on-screen counts. A stage with three 150 N pods was filled with one pod's 20 mm exit where the three are a 34.6 mm equivalent: on an 80 mm airframe under Kbf, 1,223.7 m before and 1,252.7 m now. A design saved with the one-pod figure keeps it; where the pods' motors have a published exit, the field shows the equivalent beside yours with a button to use it. The motor-room estimate searched the whole stage, so a pod's nose cone stopped the core motor: a dual-deploy core with 1.00 m to its av-bay floor read 0.45 m once a two-pod ring was added. The core's room is 1.00 m again.
Picking one catalogue part and then another kept the first part's drag, weight and fill. A Fruity Chutes IFC-030-S followed by an Apogee 29093 flew a Cd of 2.09 and landed at 1.14 m/s, where the 29093 picked fresh flies the 0.80 default and lands at 1.84 m/s. A catalogue part with a mass, picked on an assembly whose weight covers everything inside it, replaced the whole assembly's weight. A design saved after such a swap still carries the leftover: pick the part again from the catalogue, and on a part other than a canopy clear its Mass field. If a pick replaced a weight you typed for a whole assembly, type it again with "Use instead of everything inside" ticked.
Show charts and the flight-data download could re-fly a saved run at the wrong ejection delay after any Launch with the delay set to Auto (optimal) ticked. A CSV OR XLSX DOWNLOADED THAT WAY HOLDS THE WRONG FLIGHT: download it again.
Saving a design with its motor eject on Auto wrote the motor's provisional delay beside the Auto flight's numbers - a 3 s delay beside 280.4 m and a 3.81 m/s deployment, where the 3 s flight is 269.0 m and 16.81 m/s. The top motor on Auto is now saved at the delay Auto flies. To keep a fixed delay in the file, untick Auto and type the delay you want.
A top motor whose data lists no ejection delay started on 0 s, which is a charge at burnout. It now starts on Auto. The "no ejection charge" codes in RASP files are read as plugged rather than as seconds, where the report had printed "Flown delay 1000s vs optimal 7.3s". And a motor's delays are now sorted, so a fresh pick starts on the longest. These are the delays a motor starts on when you pick it; a design already saved keeps its delay.
Batch simulate now flies what the design page flies, with two exceptions. Swept with "optimal delay per motor" unticked, a plugged motor flew a 0 s charge. It now flies plugged, unless the design opens its chute on the ejection charge, where that row flies the optimum instead and is marked "· opt.". That is the first exception. The other: in a combination swept with the box unticked, a motor that lists no delay still fires its charge at burnout when it flies beside one that does. After a Check thrustcurve.org, Batch still swept the shipped catalogue; it sweeps the checked one now. And combination rows name each motor's manufacturer.
116 catalogue motors that were locked now fly, on the masses their data files state.
A rod angle or length, wind, site altitude or latitude read from an .ork or .CDX1 is now brought inside the Launch panel's own range, with a note quoting the file's value. Desktop OR allows a rod tilt up to 60 degrees either way, so a desktop file with a rail tilted more than 30 degrees now flies at 30 here.
A desktop .ork that turns off "Always launch directly up-wind or down-wind" and leans a tilted rod in another direction now flies the lean the file states, where the app flew it straight into the wind.

Saving, re-opening, undo and printing
A design saved and opened again could come back in different air. An .ork stores the launch atmosphere as both numbers or neither, and a blank beside a typed value was written as the sea-level standard: at 2,682 m with 30 °C typed and pressure blank, the flight flies 0.83878 kg/m³ and the saved file re-opened, here and in desktop OR, at 1.16439. The file now states the air the flight flies.
A .rkt you save now carries every motor. RockSim lists one engine set per motor tube, and the app wrote a cluster or a pod set with one engine set on the first copy, so the file named one motor where the design flies several. A cluster with its mass overridden was written with the whole mass on every tube, so a 300 g ring of three went into the file as 900 g. Ignition timing is now written the way RockSim counts it.
Ctrl+Z on an earlier edit after a motor change put the old motor's nozzle exit back. On the 38-54 2-stage RASAero test file, swapping the booster's K627 for an M1350 and then undoing an unrelated edit flew the K627's 22.86 mm exit under the M1350, 7.55 percent low on Kbf and 7.81 percent low on Supersonic. Undo now makes the same nozzle decision for the loaded motor that the motor change made. Separately, Ctrl+Z after an Open or a share link put the previous airframe back under the new file's motors, and Save wrote the mix; an Open, a share link and a configuration switch now start the undo history over, and a configuration switch no longer replaces the nozzle a RASAero simulation states. A recovery, separation or nozzle edit made while one flight configuration was active is no longer lost on switching away and back.
New kept the old rocket's Measured mass & CG, so a pad mass typed for the new rocket was split against the old one's weight. New clears it now, and its confirmation says Ctrl+Z does not bring it back.
Printed and cut parts now match the part the simulation flies. The paper fin template took the root chord as the rearmost point of the outline: root 100 mm, tip 50 mm, sweep 80 mm was captioned "root 130.0 mm", and a 60 mm tab was drawn at 35-95 mm where the STL and DXF cut it at 20-80 mm, so a tab cut from paper did not seat. A bulkhead or ring inside an automatic-size coupler - every coupler the Add menu makes - or inside a nose cone or transition was printed and cut 24 mm across on any airframe; on a 3-inch airframe that bulkhead is now 73.20 mm. If you printed a fin template or a bulkhead from the app, check it against the design.
The app has less to load before it draws, but comes at a first-time use download cost
The user guide and the What's new changelog now download the first time you open them instead of on every visit, so the script the app loads before it draws is 28 percent smaller (667 KB compressed, was 927 KB). The offline copy still stores both. If one fails to download, its window says so and offers Reload the page.

Crashes, lost work and accidental edits
Two open tabs no longer overwrite each other's work. The autosave is one slot shared by every open tab and the last tab to write won, so a single Launch in one tab could overwrite another tab's unsaved work, and that work was gone once that tab closed or reloaded. A tab now checks the slot before it writes, and if another tab has changed it, holds its own changes back and asks which design to keep.
An error no longer locks you out of your design. An error while drawing unmounted the whole app, and when the design itself caused it, the autosave restored that design on every reload, so the only way out was clearing site data - which also destroyed saved runs and imported motors. A recovery panel now offers the design as an .ork, and a fresh start that clears only the autosaved design, keeping runs, imported motors, custom presets and preferences. A report, plot, drag chart or run history that fails now fails in its own panel, with Try again.
Selecting a run saved before v0.099 (3 September) that had a recovery device crashed the whole app to "Something went wrong". It opens now. A tube-fin length of zero, or a zero-height camera shroud with neither end streamlined, failed the whole build, and the autosave kept it, so it failed again on every load; both now stop at 0.1 mm. When a design cannot be built, the message now names the likely part.
The 500-run limit on saved runs now says how many old runs it removed - a 226-motor sweep on top of 300 hand-flown runs removed 26 of them without a word. Clear all asks first, and a run's ✕ can be undone.
Ordinary clicks no longer change the design without saying so. A click on the 2D side view could move the part under it - 2 px of hand jitter moved a fin set 1.5 mm - and nothing moves now until the pointer travels 4 px. Clicking the words "Surface finish" pressed the "→ all" button inside the label and rewrote the finish, and so the skin-friction drag, of every part in the design. A spinner click left its value behind for the next part selected: tube B showed tube A's 45.1 g in its mass-override box, and one click up put 45.2 g on tube B. On a blank field the spinner stepped from zero, so one click on a blank Cd override replaced the part's whole computed drag with 0.05. Numeric fields also accept a decimal comma ("1,5") now.
Smaller fixes
The launch report's simulator-warning text now distinguishes its threshold from the report's own check. The simulator flags any deployment faster than 20 m/s (65.6 ft/s), and the app labelled it "risk of a zippered tube or torn chute", so an opening between 20 and 21.3 m/s read "Safe deployment: yes" and "risk of a zippered tube" in the same report. The label now names the 20 m/s threshold and says it is stricter than the report's own check. The sizing panel and the report's comment call 70 ft/s the preferred drogue limit and 70 to 90 the caution band, but the Verdict still disagrees within that band.
The data header on an image export printed a CP from one roll plane beside a margin measured from another. A cluster turned with its rotation setting was drawn, printed and written to .rkt turned the opposite way from the simulator; no flown number moved. The XLSX export follows all of Excel's tab-name rules, where some names made Excel report the workbook as corrupt, and a name pasted with a control character no longer makes a saved file that neither this app nor desktop OR will open.
An EX motor import that did not fit in the browser's storage said it had worked, but the motors could not fly even in that session. The app now empties its cache of downloaded curves and tries again, and if they still do not fit, says they are not saved and flies them until the page closes. A two-pulse .eng curve imports whole, and a UTF-16 .ork or .CDX1 opens.
The launch report calls the default model Rogers Modified Barrowman (Kbf), as Preferences does, and labels the recommended delay "drill to fit", because it is the rounded optimum and is never snapped to the delays the motor is sold with. The run-table and flight-data CSVs now open a Save As dialog in Chrome, where they had always gone straight to Downloads.
Keyboard and screen reader: the motor table was one Tab stop per row - 232 on a 29 mm mount - and is one stop now, walked with the arrow keys; the EX-motor and preset-CSV import buttons can be reached from the keyboard; and after Launch a screen reader hears "Flight complete" with the apogee. In the light theme the red, amber and green safety text fell as low as 2.93 to 1 contrast against its background, under the 4.5 minimum; it now measures no lower than 4.62.
A motor whose thrust curve adds up to more than 5 percent away from its certified total impulse was announced as a curve that "needed repair" because of "a fault in the motor file". Nothing had been repaired: 144 files in the collection showed that message and none needed a repair. It now stands as its own warning in its own words, and no number moves.
Dragging a part in the 2D side view now moves only the drawing until you let go, then changes the design once - one undo step. The cost: the CG and CP markers and the stat tiles change when you let go, not while the part slides. An altitude typed into the Drag analysis panel takes effect on Enter or when you leave the box, instead of re-running the sweep on every keystroke.
Converting a one-fin set to a camera shroud made a fairing that weighs nothing when the set's outline crossed itself and came from an .ork or a share link; it now takes a stand-in 80 by 20 mm box, and the conversion note asks you to check its mass and shape. In Daylight mode, five confirmation dialogs now darken the page behind them as the other dialogs do.
The OOP badge
The motor browser no longer labels motors that are still made as OOP. It already treated the 27 motors thrustcurve.org lists as produced occasionally as available, but still put OOP beside them. Those badges are gone; the 340 out-of-production motors keep theirs.
